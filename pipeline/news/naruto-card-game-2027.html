Bandai is bringing Naruto back to trading card games. The new NARUTO CARD GAME launches worldwide in June 2027, more than a decade after Bandai's original Naruto CCG ended. Here is everything announced so far, and how it connects to the cards on Jonin.

Release date and what launches first

Bandai revealed the launch date at New York Comic Con on October 10, 2026: the game releases in June 2027, in every region at once. Three starter decks come out at launch, each built around a different leader (Naruto, Sasuke or Gaara) and ready to play out of the box. The first booster pack, “Enter: Naruto Uzumaki!”, releases alongside them and covers characters from Naruto and Naruto Shippuden.

How to try it before launch

  • Premier Demo Events start in February 2027 at participating card shops. The first wave is select large stores in Bandai's TCG+ program, and more stores join in a second wave. Store lists and sign-ups haven't been announced yet.
  • 1 million free Demo Decks will be handed out worldwide starting in spring 2027.
  • Two event-only items, an official “Naruto vs. Sasuke” playmat and a “Premium Card Collection Vol.00 – Chakra Set”, are only available at the demo events and some Bandai Card Games Fest 26-27 stops.
  • Bandai says the next big update comes at Jump Festa 2027 in December.

How it plays

Bandai describes it as a strategic, competitive card game. Coverage of the official trailer reports that each player uses a 51-card deck plus five Chakra cards and one Summon card. Full rules haven't been published yet, so we'll update this page when they are.

New game vs. the original Naruto CCG

This isn't Bandai's first Naruto card game. The original Bandai Naruto Collectible Card Game ran in English from 2006 to 2013, starting with The Path to Hokage and ending with Ultimate Ninja Storm 3. That's the game Jonin catalogs: 28 main sets plus tournament packs, collector tins and promos, over 4,300 cards.

The 2027 game is a brand-new game with its own cards and rules. Bandai hasn't said anything about the old cards working with it, and the deck format is different, so treat them as two separate games. It's also separate from the Kayou Naruto cards sold in recent years, which come from a different company.
